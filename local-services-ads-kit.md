## 1. What LSA is and why it sits above everything

Local Services Ads are Google's **pay-per-lead** (not pay-per-click) product for service businesses. Key structural facts that make it different from the Search campaign:

- **Placement:** LSA units render at the **very top of the results page — above Search/PPC ads, above the Map pack, above organic.** A contractor on LSA can out-position a competitor spending 10× on regular Search ads.
- **Trust badge:** Verified providers earn the **Google Guaranteed** badge (green checkmark). For general contractors this is *Google Guaranteed*, not *Google Screened* (Screened is for professional services like lawyers/financial planners). Google also backs Guaranteed jobs with a customer money-back guarantee up to a capped amount (lifetime cap, region-dependent) — a real trust lever for a high-ticket buyer.
- **You pay per lead, not per click.** A "lead" = a phone call (over a minimum duration), a message, or a booking through the unit. Bad/irrelevant leads can be disputed for credit (see §7).
- **Ranking is NOT an auction in the Search sense.** Reviews, responsiveness, proximity, and Google Guaranteed status drive who shows — money alone can't buy the top slot (see §6).

**Why this matters for Biviano:** the avatar (35–55, $150K+ HH, been quoted $1M+, scared of "modular = trailer") responds to trust signals. The Google Guaranteed badge + 5-star reviews showing inside the LSA unit is exactly that signal, delivered above the fold before anyone clicks.

## 2. Eligibility — which category fits, and the honest fit problem

### 2a. The category
LSA's eligible Home Services categories include **General Contractor**, defined by Google as *"a service provider who provides material and builds services necessary for building construction."* That is the category Biviano signs up under.

- There is **no dedicated "Modular Home Builder" or "New Home Builder" LSA category.** "General Contractor" is the closest and correct bucket.
- Within the category you select **specific job types** (e.g., remodels, additions, deck/porch, kitchen/bath, repairs, new construction where offered). **Select job types you actually want and can service** — Google bills you for leads that match your selected job types and service area, and (see §7) it has **stopped crediting "job type not serviced" / "geo not serviced" disputes**, so loose settings = paying for junk.

### 2b. Where LSA may NOT fit new-home construction (be honest with Mike)
- The "General Contractor" category's demand pool skews **home-improvement / remodel / repair / addition** — homeowners searching for a contractor for an existing-property project. That is precisely the HIC-registration world (MA Ch. 142A: work on existing owner-occupied 1–4 unit homes).
- **Net-new ground-up modular homes are a small, atypical slice** of that demand. Most LSA contacts will be remodel/addition/repair shoppers, with new-build inquiries the exception.
- **Implication:** LSA is best framed for Biviano as a **GC-brand lead source that monetizes the existing Biviano General Contracting LLC capability** (remodels, additions, decks, kitchens/baths — all things they genuinely do and have testimonials for), while the **modular new-build pipeline stays driven by the Search campaign + dedicated LP.** Don't promise Mike a flood of modular-home leads from LSA. Promise cheap, badge-backed GC leads that keep crews busy and fund brand presence.

> **Recommendation:** Sign up LSA under **Biviano General Contracting LLC** (the licensed legal entity) — it owns the license, insurance, reviews, and GBP — and select job types Biviano wants (additions, remodels, decks, kitchen/bath, repairs, and new construction). Route the leads into the same GHL pipeline, tag them `lsa` so we can measure them separately.

## 4. Pricing model + how to set a weekly budget for ~$2k/mo blended

### 4a. How LSA bills
- **You pay per valid lead** (qualifying call/message/booking), not per click or impression.
- You control spend two ways:
  1. **Weekly budget** — the max you'll spend in a 7-day cycle. Google may spend up to ~2× your weekly budget in a single high-demand week but **averages out to your set weekly amount over the month**; ads pause when the budget is exhausted, resume next cycle.
  2. **(Optional) Max per-lead bid / "Maximize Leads" mode** — let Google optimize, or cap what you'll pay per lead. At launch, run **"Maximize Leads"** so Google gathers data; revisit after 30 days.

### 4b. The ~$2k/mo target — and the honest split decision
The campaign blueprint already allocates **~$2,000/mo to paid Search.** LSA is a *separate* budget. Two ways to play it:

- **Option A — LSA is additive (recommended if Mike has appetite):** keep Search at ~$2k/mo and add an LSA weekly budget on top. General-contractor LSA CPLs vary widely (often **~$30–$80 per lead for smaller GC/remodel jobs**, higher for larger-project leads). A **$300–$500/mo LSA budget ≈ $70–$115/week** is a sensible, low-risk test that yields roughly **5–15 leads/mo** at GC CPLs.
- **Option B — LSA inside the existing $2k blended:** carve, e.g., **$1,500/mo Search + $500/mo LSA**. Cheaper to start, but it dilutes the Search campaign that drives actual modular new-builds. **Only do this if total budget is hard-capped at $2k.**

**Recommended starting weekly budget:** **$90–$115/week (~$400–$500/mo)** for LSA. Reasoning:
- Low enough to be a true test that can't blow up.
- High enough to clear Google's "active enough to rank" bar.
- At ~$30–$80 GC CPL, that's a steady **~6–15 leads/mo** — meaningful for keeping crews busy without overwhelming Mike's intake.

**Set it like this in-product:** Leads → Budget → **Weekly budget = $100** (adjust ±). Turn ON "Maximize Leads" for the first 30 days. Set **service area** tightly (see §6) and select **only job types Biviano wants** so you don't pay for unservable leads.

## 6. How LSA ranking works (reviews, responsiveness, proximity)

Money does not buy the top slot. Google ranks LSA providers primarily on:

1. **Review score + volume (heaviest lever).** Star rating and number of Google reviews shown in the unit. **Action: actively migrate/grow Google reviews.** Biviano has **21 five-star reviews on Birdeye** — those don't count for LSA; **only reviews on the Google Business Profile count.** Run a campaign to get those happy clients to re-post on Google. Google also lets LSA providers **request reviews via a special LSA review link** — use it after every job.
2. **Responsiveness.** Answer-rate and speed-to-respond on LSA calls/messages. Missed calls tank ranking and waste paid leads. **Action: Mike (or a dispatcher/VA) must answer LSA calls fast, every time, during set business hours.** Set realistic LSA business hours so the profile only shows when someone can answer.
3. **Proximity / service area.** Closer jobs rank higher. Set the **service area by the towns Biviano actually serves** — Marshfield, Scituate, Duxbury, Hanover, Pembroke, Norwell, Hingham, Humarock, Plymouth County. **Set by ZIPs/towns, not a wide radius** — a too-wide area pulls unservable leads you'll now pay for (no geo credit anymore, see §7).
4. **Google Guaranteed status + profile completeness + responsiveness/dispute history.** Verified, complete, well-behaved profiles rank above incomplete ones.
5. **Budget adequacy.** Not a ranking *boost*, but if your weekly budget is exhausted you stop showing — so an underfunded profile effectively ranks nowhere late in the week.

**Ranking playbook for Biviano:** (1) drive Google reviews relentlessly via the LSA review link, (2) answer every LSA call within seconds during business hours, (3) tight town-level service area, (4) keep the profile 100% complete with photos (use the finished-home + crane-set photos), (5) keep the weekly budget funded so the profile shows all week.

## 7. Disputing bad leads (credit process) — and the 2026 reality

You can **dispute leads that aren't legitimate** and Google credits valid disputes (the lead cost comes back, not cash).

**Currently creditable dispute reasons:**
- **Spam / bot / not a real customer.**
- **Wrong number / no actual service request** (e.g., a job seeker, a vendor solicitation, a misdial).
- **Duplicate** of a lead you already paid for.
- **Customer was not seeking your service** in a way that's clearly outside what was advertised (narrower than it used to be — see below).

**How to dispute:** in the **Local Services Ads dashboard → the lead → "Dispute" / report → select reason → submit** (often must include note/recording). **Dispute promptly** — there's a submission window (a few days after the lead).

**Honest 2026 changes Mike must know:**
- **Google removed credits for "job type not serviced" and "geo not serviced"** (rolled out 2024–2025). If your **service area or job-type settings are loose, you pay for those leads — no credit.** This is why §6's tight service-area + job-type selection is now load-bearing, not optional.
- **Dispute resolution is slower than it used to be** — contractors report **multi-week turnarounds (3–4 weeks)** in 2026 vs the old ~48 hours. Build the habit of disputing every junk lead anyway; credits add up.
- **Maintain a high answer rate** — disputing leads you *missed* (didn't answer) is weak; Google expects you to pick up.

## 8. How LSA complements the Search campaign

| Layer | Product | Position on SERP | Pricing | Best at |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Top** | **Local Services Ads** | Above everything | Pay per lead | Cheapest CPL; trust badge; broad GC/remodel demand + occasional new-build |
| **Middle** | **Search campaign** (the blueprint) | Below LSA, above organic | Pay per click | High-intent **modular new-build** terms → dedicated LP → booked consults |
| **Base** | **SEO** (keyword map) | Organic + Map pack | Free (earned) | Research-stage + long-tail town terms |

- **They don't cannibalize — they stack.** A homeowner searching "modular home builder south shore" can see Biviano's **LSA unit (badge + reviews) at the very top AND the Search ad (LP message-match) right below.** Two trust-reinforcing impressions for one searcher.
- **LSA usually has the lowest contractor CPL**, so it's the efficient floor of the funnel. Search buys the *specific high-intent modular* clicks LSA's category can't target precisely.
- **Shared pipeline:** both feed GHL; both measured to **booked consult**. Compare cost-per-booked-consult LSA vs Search monthly and shift budget to whichever produces better *modular* pipeline — but expect LSA to win volume/CPL and Search to win new-build quality.
- **Reviews compound across both:** growing the Google review count lifts LSA rank *and* the Search ads' seller rating/extensions *and* Map pack. One review push, three wins.
